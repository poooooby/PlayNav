<h1 align="center">NaviPlay</h1>

<div align="center">

*A second screen for your Pokémon. Pet them, feed them Poké Puffs, play games with them and
train them, on a DS-style touch screen in Red, Blue, Yellow, Gold, Silver, Crystal, Ruby,
Sapphire, Emerald, FireRed and LeafGreen.*

</div>

A mod for [Gen1Recomp](https://github.com/bryanthaboi/gen1recomp)'s Generation 1, 2 and 3 games. It
brings **Pokémon-Amie** and **Super Training** from X, Y, Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire, played
on a touch screen below the game or on a device's second display.

## ✨ Features

### Core Mechanics

- 📱 **A touch screen of your own:** It appears only when you call NaviPlay, laid out exactly as
  Kanto Gear lays out its own screen, with the same Display Mode choices: **Fullscreen Swap**
  (NaviPlay or the game fills the window, Y swaps them), **Combined Screen** (stacked, side by side,
  or a panel over the game) or **Separate Screens** (a second display, such as the AYN Thor's).
  With **K-Gear** it simply follows Kanto Gear's settings, so switching between the two is
  seamless. The touch screen is always drawn at your display's full sharpness.
- 🧸 **Pokémon-Amie:** Spend time with your Pokémon to raise its affection, and it fights better
  for you.
- 🏋️ **Super Training:** Raise your Pokémon's EVs with training bags and shooting regimens.
- 🖼️ **Your choice of look and sound:** the first time you open NaviPlay its screen asks.
  **Begin extraction** builds the art, music, sound effects and English text from your own Alpha
  Sapphire or Omega Ruby copy, for the real 3DS look. **Use fallback assets** needs no copy:
  every screen is drawn by NaviPlay itself, with its own sounds and words and the GBA games' own
  songs for music. Switch between the two any time with **USE ROM ASSETS**.
- 🧭 **The NaviPlay menu:** **NAVIPLAY** on the START menu opens NaviPlay on the touch screen,
  with a card for each app (**Pokémon-Amie** and **Super Training**) as the game's PlayNav lists
  them. Tap one to open it; closing an app brings you back to the menu, and the x (or NAVIPLAY
  again) closes it.
- 📳 **Haptics:** On a phone or handheld you feel your touches: a tick for menu presses, a
  pulse for a good stroke, a bite or a hit that lands, two knocks when your Pokémon doesn't like
  it or you miss, and a burst for a cleared bag, a finished puzzle or a popped Balloon Bot. Set
  how strong in the options, or turn it off.
- 🔍 **Sharp at any size:** Both screens are drawn at your display's full resolution. The 3DS
  art stays smooth at any size, Pokémon sprites keep their square pixels, and text stays crisp.
  A line too long for its box shrinks to fit. The overworld, battles and the rest of the game
  look exactly as before.

### Pokémon-Amie 💕

- 🏠 **The Amie room:** **Pokémon-Amie** in the NaviPlay menu opens a room where your Pokémon wanders,
  plays and naps. It's laid out from the game's own screen, scalloped frame and all. Tap your
  Pokémon for **PLAY**, **DECORATE** or **SWITCH** (another party member); while the menu is
  up, bubbles over it show how much it wants Poké Puffs and play, and how fond of you it is.
- 🌤️ **Play:** your Pokémon stands large on a lawn under the sky, its back to you until you tap
  it. It moves with its mood: lively and hopping when it's happy, drooping when it's hungry,
  and an unwell Pokémon shivers and goes back to its ball. Leave it alone long enough and it
  dozes off. Turn on **3D AMIE** and it stands on the game's own 3D stage instead (from your
  card image, or a drawn one without it): the lawn, a dotted sky, a rainbow, drifting clouds
  and rising bubbles, framed by the game's camera for its species, with the top screen looking
  up into the same sky.
- 🤚 **Petting:** Stroke your Pokémon with your finger and it wriggles along with music notes
  (hearts on a spot it loves); keep at it and it hops for joy. Every species has a spot it
  loves and one it dislikes (it flinches away), and some have spots you shouldn't touch at
  all: Charmander's tail burns, Pikachu's cheeks shock, Regice freezes.
- 🧁 **Poké Puffs:** Open the puff drawer (swipe it sideways to see them all; a number shows
  how many of a kind you have), pull a puff out and hold it for your Pokémon: it eats while you
  keep it at its mouth, bite by bite, in as many bites as its species takes. Let go and the
  puff falls away and is lost, though one it hasn't bitten yet can go back in the drawer.
  There are 26 kinds, from Basic to Supreme, and you win them in the minigames. A nearly full
  Pokémon eats slowly, and a few, like Metapod and Shedinja, can't eat.
- ✋ **Little moments:** Hold a finger out beside a fond Pokémon and it leaps up to high-five
  your hand. Tap twice too fast and it feels hit. It dances as its affection grows and leaves
  you a present at each affection milestone.
- 🔊 **Sound effects:** Amie plays a sound for every moment, at the game's effects volume:
  petting, eating, waking and dozing, the high five, DECORATE, visitors coming and going, and
  every minigame moment from START! to the HIGH SCORE stamp. With your card image they're the
  3DS game's own; without it, NaviPlay's, shaped after them.
- 🎵 **Minigame music:** each minigame has its own theme, following the game's music settings.
  With your card image it's the 3DS game's (Berry Picker's speeds up as time runs out, and Head
  It's rises a key in FEVER); without it, a song from your GBA game. Play and the room keep your
  area's music, as the game does.
- 🎮 **Controller:** wherever NaviPlay has the screen you can also play with the pad: A wakes
  your Pokémon and opens the minigames, SELECT the Poké Puff drawer; the minigame and regimen
  pickers take the D-pad and A; Head It heads with left / up / right, Tile Puzzle moves a
  cursor; end buttons and results take A and B. Feeding, petting and Berry Picker are touch.
- 🎯 **Three minigames,** picked with the Play button on the Play screen, by touch or with the
  controller (left / right to choose, A to pick, B to step back):
  - **Berry Picker:** hand each Pokémon the berry it asks for, on the game's own screens: the opening
    demo, the tree and the thought bubbles, TIME'S UP and the berries raining onto the heap, the Poké
    Puffs you won, Play Again or Quit, and the RESULTS card on the top screen.
  - **Head It:** balls of yarn fall from the top screen onto the Pokémon they're meant for; tap
    it as the ball reaches the star over its head to head it back. Up to three Pokémon join in,
    the yarn speeds up as you keep heading it, a long combo brings FEVER (double points), and
    time up drops a giant ball of yarn for a big bonus before the RESULTS card.
  - **Tile Puzzle:** swap tiles to rebuild a moving picture of your Pokémon in the sea, on the game's
    own screens: the whole picture and Begin, the tiles flying apart, START!, blue corners on the
    tiles out of place and red on the one you pick, stars as a tile lands home (it locks there),
    then the RESULTS card with your three best times, the Poké Puffs you won, and View Picture,
    Play Again or Quit. The picture turns from day to sunset to night and back as you play; on
    Easy the Pokémon bob in place, on Normal they move about, and on Hard the view drifts too.

  Each game has Easy, Normal, Hard and Unlimited ranks, star ratings and Poké Puff prizes.
- 🛋️ **Décor and visitors:** Decorate the room with any of 34 wallpapers and 172 cushions and
  objects, on the game's own DECORATE screen: slide through your wallpapers with the arrows at
  the top, open **Interior Items** or **Poké Puffs**, and drag what you pick into the room. Let
  it go near one of the spots around the middle and it settles exactly on it; tap a placed item
  to move it again, or drop it in the tray to put it back. Set out a Poké Puff on the middle spot
  (it falls into place when you're back in the room) and visiting Pokémon from your Pokédex drop
  by now and then; a better puff, and Décor on the seats around it, make them likelier to stay.
  Three that stay chat together and leave you a present, picked by who they are and how fond
  they are. After you enter the Hall of Fame, the next three groups each bring a Supreme Honor
  Poké Puff.
- ⚔️ **Affection in battle:** The fonder your Pokémon, the more it does for you. At two hearts
  it earns more experience. From three it can hang on at 1 HP. From four it can shake off a
  status. At 200 affection foes miss it more, and at five hearts it lands more critical hits.
  From four hearts it also talks to you in battle, in the game's own words: cheering a
  super-effective hit, worrying when its HP runs low, delighted when you heal it, dozing when
  asleep.

### Super Training 🎈

- 🥊 **Core Training:** **Super Training** in the NaviPlay menu puts your Pokémon beside a punching bag on
  the touch screen while you keep exploring on the top screen (the blue return button goes back
  to the menu; NAVIPLAY on the START menu closes it all), with the Effort-o-Meter for its base stats and EVs. Tap anywhere in the room to punch the
  bag, one punch at a time. Your Pokémon also punches the bag once a minute on its own, even with the screen
  closed.
- 😴 **Resting:** After half an hour to an hour, or every 3 to 5 bags it clears, whichever comes
  first, it lies down until you tap it. A fainted Pokémon stays down until it is revived.
- 😄 **Second wind:** Woken from a rest, after some cleared bags, from a rare find or the Team
  Flare Bag, it gets a happy bubble and punches with double strength: the next bag takes half
  the taps, or the next regimen scores double. It lasts one bag or one regimen.
- 🎁 **The blank bag:** With no training bag set, every punch (yours or your Pokémon's) has a 5%
  chance of digging up a bag, 10% for a Pokémon with all 510 EVs; any bag but the Team Flare
  Bag can turn up, each as likely as the next. A find plays the game's presentation: the new
  bag drops down for a look while your Pokémon celebrates, then it's announced and racked.
- 👜 **26 training bags:** Stat bags in three sizes (+1, +4 and +12 EVs), the Reset Bag, the
  Soothing Bag, and six bags that power up your next regimen. Tap a bag to read about it, then
  use or discard it.
- 🎯 **Super-Training Regimens:** Shoot at the goals on a big Balloon Bot made in the image of a
  real Pokémon (every one of them, from Axew to Mega Aggron, drawn from its own sprite), in a
  stadium, the way the game plays it: the course's title, the balloon pumped
  up on the grass, both names, **Start**, and **Success!** as the balloon zips off into the sky.
  On split screen the picker, the stadium and the results fill the whole top of the screen, as
  the overworld does.
  - **Controls:** tap the touch screen to shoot where you touch (the brackets on both screens
    line up). Hold to charge: a Charged Shot scores up to triple points, but held too long it
    fizzles and leaves your Pokémon dazed for a moment. Hold L or R for a shield that bounces a
    ball back and then shatters. A mark on the top screen shows where your last shot went.
    Regimens always run at normal speed, whatever the game speed is set to. The **CONTROLS** tab
    in the regimen picker (or SELECT) shows them all on the top screen, on a drawn handheld.
  - **Moving:** your Pokémon rides a hover board. Steer it across and up and down with the
    d-pad, anywhere on the screen.
  - **3D stadium (option):** turn on **3D SUPER TRAINING** to play in a real 3D stadium - the
    game's own, from your card image, or a drawn one without it - as the game plays it: your
    Pokémon circles the balloon as you move, the camera riding with it, framed as the 3DS frames
    it on any screen shape, and a ripple of light flashes beside it when you reach an edge.
    Step nearer or farther with the right stick or the mouse wheel (the game's three
    distances): nearer, your shots land sooner, but so do the balls. The balloons, balls,
    bitbots and shields are the game's sizes, and a cleared regimen ends as the game's does:
    the last hit swells the balloon, a flash, and from low on the grass you watch it squeeze
    thin, burst and spiral away into the sky.
  - **The energy meter** fills as you play; each shot spends some, and with too little left you
    fire a weak shot. Your Pokémon's Energy Shot colour sets the cost and the points.
  - **The goals** move with the balloon, which sways and bobs in its own way, and appear in
    groups that run out on a timer: white, then orange, then gone. The balloon inflates as your
    score climbs and wobbles at each hit. Clear a group and it freezes, puffing air from its
    sides, before a red goal that takes several hits.
  - **The balloon fights back** with each course's own attacks from the game: volleys, streams,
    spreading rings and spirals, giant balls, bitbots that circle and fire, and a shield it
    hides behind until you hit the target circling it (Graveler, Golem). A magenta glow warns
    of a big attack. Blue balls home in on you, but slower than you move: keep moving, or juke,
    and they miss. Wide and tall balls home the same way; they're just harder to get round.
  - **What you see is what hits:** a ball hits your Pokémon only if it visibly touches it.
    On the flat view, where each ball will cross your line shows as a shadow on the grass with a
    ring closing in, red and blinking if it's going to hit you where you stand; the 3D stadium
    shows no warnings, as the game doesn't.
  - **Circuits:** on courses with more than one balloon the clock stops when one is beaten; it
    collapses onto the grass, the next inflates where it lies, and 30 seconds go on the clock.
  - **Your EVs count, the game's way:** Attack and Sp. Atk power your shots, Speed makes them
    and you faster (a fully trained shot crosses the field in a quarter of a second), HP
    makes them bigger and softens the hits you take, and Defense and Sp. Def refill the meter
    faster and hold the shield longer. Every EV you have softens hits a little more.
  - **The results** show which stat went up on the meter, your time, any new best and the bags
    you won, with the coach's words a tap at a time.
  - **Music:** the regimen picker, every regimen and its results have their own music in place
    of the overworld's, which picks up again when you go back: Super Training's own with your
    card image, else songs from your GBA game (and Wooble's own track for the picker). It follows
    the game's sound settings: music volume, audio mode and music filter.
  - **Sound effects:** every moment has its sound, at the game's effects volume: from the
    Regimens button and the balloon's pumps, through every shot, hit and attack, to the balloon
    zipping off as a star and the results. With your card image they're the 3DS game's own.
- 🏆 **30 regimens:** 18 regular courses over three levels, each with the game's own name,
  description, Balloon Bot and score. Tap one to see its card on the top screen. Clear one
  within the target time for an **Award**.
- 🌟 **Secret regimens:** At 510 EVs your Pokémon is **Fully Trained** and twelve secret
  regimens open: multi-bot circuits, sudden death, raging and calming bots, and prizes such as
  Wings, evolution stones, PP Up and PP Max. An Award on every regimen makes it **Supremely
  Trained**.
- 🪶 **Items from later games:** The six stat Wings, the Pretty Wing and the Dusk, Shiny and Dawn
  Stones join the game, with their own icons and descriptions. A Wing used from the bag raises one EV by 1.

## ⚙️ Options

Open **OPTIONS → MODS → NaviPlay**.

| Option | Choices | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AFFECTION BENEFITS** | On / Off | On | Affection's bonuses in battle. Off for vanilla battles. |
| **DISPLAY MODE** | K-Gear / Fullscreen Swap / Combined Screen / Separate Screens | K-Gear | Where the touch screen goes when you call NaviPlay (the START menu's NAVIPLAY or Kanto Gear's apps; it never shows otherwise). The same choices and settings as Kanto Gear's Display Mode, so every dual-screen mod reads alike. **K-Gear** uses Kanto Gear's own settings for all of this (the rows below hide), so you set it once in the Gear; without Kanto Gear it uses a second display when there is one, else Combined Screen. |
| **START SCREEN** | Game / NaviPlay | NaviPlay | Fullscreen Swap: which one shows first when you call NaviPlay. Y (F6 on a keyboard) swaps them. |
| **LAYOUT** | Auto / Stacked / Side by Side / Overlay | Auto | Combined Screen: the game and NaviPlay one above the other, side by side, or NaviPlay as a panel over the game. |
| **PRIMARY VIEW** | Game / NaviPlay | Game | Combined Screen: which one gets the larger part. |
| **SECONDARY SIZE** | Auto / 20% – 80% | Auto | Combined Screen: how much of the window the smaller part takes. |
| **BOTTOM SAFE AREA** | Off / 10% – 40% | Off | Combined Screen: keep the bottom of the window free for touch controls (a phone's on-screen buttons). |
| **OVERLAY CORNER** | Top Left / Top Right / Bottom Left / Bottom Right | Bottom Right | Combined Screen, Overlay: where the panel sits. |
| **OVERLAY BUTTON** | Off / R3 / L3 / F7 | Off | Combined Screen, Overlay: a button that hides and shows the panel. |
| **NAVIPLAY OUTPUT** | Auto / Main Screen / Second Screen | Auto | Separate Screens: which display NaviPlay uses. Main Screen keeps NaviPlay on your main screen and sends the game to the second display. |
| **QUICK SWAP (Y)** | On / Off | Off | Y (F6 on a keyboard) swaps the game and NaviPlay. |
| **HIDE VIRTUAL PAD** | On / Off | On | Hide the on-screen pad while the touch screen is in use. |
| **USE ROM ASSETS** | On / Off | On | On: the 3DS game's own art, layouts, music, sounds and text, from your Omega Ruby or Alpha Sapphire copy (nothing of NaviPlay's stands in). Off: NaviPlay's own, no copy needed. **Use fallback assets** on the NaviPlay screen turns it off; turning it back on checks for your copy again. |
| **EXTRACT OR/AS ASSETS ON BOOT** | On / Off | On | Once you have chosen to extract, resume or start the unpacking automatically at the next start. |
| **CLEAR EXTRACTED ART** | Press To Clear | Press To Clear | Press it to delete the unpacked art, music, sound and text; the NaviPlay screen then shows **Begin extraction** to unpack them again, or the missing-card warning if the card image is no longer imported. In the game it clears at once and reads Press To Clear again; in the launcher it reads Clears At Next Start until you're in the game (press it again to undo). |
| **3D AMIE** | On / Off | Off | Play with your Pokémon in Pokémon-Amie on the game's own 3D stage, both screens, with the game's camera for its species. With your card image it's the game's own stage; without it, a drawn one. Off, or on a device that can't draw it: the flat lawn and sky. |
| **3D SUPER TRAINING** | On / Off | Off | Play Super-Training Regimens in a real 3D stadium as the game does: you circle the balloon, the camera riding with you; the right stick or the mouse wheel steps nearer or farther. With your card image it's the game's own stadium; without it, a drawn one. Off, or on a device that can't draw it: the flat view. |
| **HD RENDERING** | On / Off | On | Draw both screens at your display's full resolution. Off: the plain low-resolution look. |
| **GEN 1/2 SPRITES** | NaviPlay / Modded | NaviPlay | Red, Blue, Yellow, Gold, Silver and Crystal: NaviPlay draws your Pokémon with its own full-colour Crystal sprites. **Modded** uses a sprite mod's art instead (such as G9 Battle Sprites), front and back, wherever that mod has art for a Pokémon, and NaviPlay's own wherever it has none. |
| **HAPTICS** | High / Medium / Low / Off | Medium | How strongly your phone or handheld vibrates for touches and play: menu presses, petting, bites, high fives, minigame hits, punching bags and Balloon Bot hits. No effect on a PC. |
| **LOW POWER MODE** | On / Off | Off | For a phone or handheld with little memory: the touch screen is sent to a second display at half the resolution, a quarter of the pixels each frame. Slightly softer there, much lighter on the device. |

## 💾 Installation

1. Download the latest release from the [Releases](https://github.com/poooooby/PlayNav/releases)
   page.
2. Import it with the Gen1Recomp Mod Manager, or drop it in your mods folder.
3. Enable **NaviPlay**.
4. **For the 3DS art and text (optional):** open the mod in the launcher, choose
   **Imported files**, then **Choose file**. Pick your own decrypted **Pokémon Omega Ruby or
   Alpha Sapphire (USA)**: a card image (`.cci` / `.3ds`, trimmed or not) or a decrypted
   `.cia`. Without one, NaviPlay plays with its own art, sounds and words. NaviPlay checks each
   piece it uses as it unpacks them, and draws its own art for any piece your copy holds
   differently. On Gen1Recomp builds from before mod-checked imports (before 0.3.96), the
   launcher accepts only this exact Alpha Sapphire file:

   | | |
   |---|---|
   | **Format** | Decrypted `.cci` |
   | **Size** | 2,147,483,648 bytes (2 GiB) |
   | **MD5** | `0563f9dee1b95de32d579e40c20e2bfc` |

   The launcher keeps a private copy. The first time NaviPlay starts, its screen
   asks: **Begin extraction** unpacks the art, music, sound effects and text with a progress
   bar (a few seconds, in the background while you play), then tells you the copy can be
   removed under Imported files; **Use fallback assets** uses NaviPlay's own instead. Without a
   copy, or with the wrong one, the screen says so (**OR/AS ROM not found** / **Incorrect ROM
   added**) and offers the fallback assets; if you add the right copy later, NaviPlay offers
   it again on the next start.

## 🔗 Works With

- [Kanto Gear](https://github.com/AverageConsumer/kanto-gear): touch battle commands, bag and
  pad. NaviPlay doesn't do controls; the two share the second display. NaviPlay has it only while
  the NaviPlay menu, Amie or Super Training is open, and Kanto Gear is back on it the moment you
  close them. Pokémon-Amie and Super Training are
  also apps in the Gear's Silph Store: get them, put them on the Gear's home screen, and open
  them from there.
- [National Dex Gen 3](https://github.com/poooooby/national_dex_gen3): the real Dusk, Shiny and
  Dawn Stones with their evolutions. Super Training's stone prizes become those stones.
- [G9 Battle Sprites (Gen 3)](https://github.com/poooooby/g9-battle-sprites-gen3): your
  Pokémon show its sprites in Pokémon-Amie and Super Training, each animated sprite held in one
  still pose.

## 📌 Good to Know

- 🧪 **Faithful, tuned for touch:** Super Training's rules, damage, attacks, balloon sizes and
  timings are read from the game's own code and data, and checked against recordings of the game;
  on the flat view a few things are tuned for a flat touch screen (bigger hitboxes you can see,
  homing you can outrun, quicker movement), while the 3D stadium plays them the game's way.
- 🎮 **Generations 1, 2 and 3.** Red, Blue, Yellow, Gold, Silver, Crystal, Ruby, Sapphire,
  Emerald, FireRed and LeafGreen. NaviPlay finds out which game it is on by itself.
- 🕹️ **On Gen 1 and 2,** Super Training works in those games' Stat Exp: a stat holds up to 252
  EVs (Stat Exp 63,504, the stat's full bonus), there is no 510 total, and Sp. Atk and Sp. Def
  bags and regimens train the one Special stat. A Pokémon is Fully Trained with all five stats
  at 252. Your Pokémon are drawn with Pokémon Crystal's sprites.
- 💾 **Your copy of the game is never part of the mod.** The 3DS art and text come from the
  card image you supply, unpacked on your own device. Encrypted dumps (a raw cartridge dump, an eShop `.cia`)
  can't be read; supply a decrypted one. Azahar's compressed `.zcci` works as it is.
- 🖱️ **On a PC with a second-display window,** strokes don't come through, only taps, so
  petting needs Split Screen there. A real second panel, like the AYN Thor's, works
  fully.
- 🤚 **Petting spots** cover broad areas (head, face, belly...) of the sprite rather than exact
  pixels, and the visitors are Pokémon from your Pokédex rather than other players'.
- 🪶 **The Wings and stones** are items the original game doesn't have. If you remove NaviPlay, a save
  that holds them shows them as numbers.
- 🔁 **Coming from DS Touch?** NaviPlay was called DS Touch in its early test builds. Your
  affection, puffs, Décor and training records carry over by themselves on the first load, and
  the art is unpacked once more.
